The iris seen from the front with its two muscles: the sphincter, circular, controlled by the parasympathetic system through cranial nerve III, the ciliary ganglion and M3 muscarinic receptors; the dilator, radial, controlled by the sympathetic system through the ciliospinal centre, the superior cervical ganglion and alpha-1 receptors. Below, the ciliary muscle seen from the side with zonule and crystalline lens: it receives almost all the parasympathetic fibres of the ciliary ganglion and a small sympathetic beta-2 branch. You can choose full or dim light, far or near gaze, eye drops or a lesion.

Pupil diameters are indicative: the drawing shows the direction and order of magnitude of the effect, not a patient's values. The sphincter is stronger than the dilator, which is why a muscarinic agonist constricts the pupil even in the dark. The share of fibres going to the ciliary muscle (about 97%) is the classic estimate from anatomical studies of the ciliary ganglion. If the ciliary ganglion is damaged, many ciliary fibres regrow onto the sphincter: Adie's tonic pupil.
