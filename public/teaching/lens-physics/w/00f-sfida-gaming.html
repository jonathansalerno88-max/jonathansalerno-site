Online shooter on a gaming TV. On the left strategy A: the game at 60 fps, the TV at 120 Hz, low-latency interpolation and HDR. On the right the best compromise: 120 fps, 120 Hz and black frame insertion. Four checks say who passes: blur, lag, flicker, clean image.

Online shooter: what counts is the blur (at most 8 px) and the lag between button and screen (at most 20 ms).

Hz = 1/s · fps = images/s · nit = cd/m²

Simplified model, orders of magnitude for a mid-range OLED TV. Persistence P: how long each image stays lit; with no tricks it is 1/fps, with interpolation 1/Hz, with BFI half the refresh. Blur = speed × P, with an object crossing 1,920 px in 2 s (960 px/s). Game-mode lag about 10 ms with a 60 Hz signal and 5 ms at 120 Hz, as in typical RTINGS measurements; low-latency interpolation (only some TVs, and without VRR) must wait for the next real frame, so it adds 1/fps; standard interpolation, outside game mode, brings lag to tens or hundreds of ms (100 here). BFI: half the refresh is black, so half the light and about half a refresh of extra lag; at 60 Hz many people see it flicker, and many TVs switch it off with HDR. If the game is slower than the TV, BFI flashes the same image twice and you see it double. HDR peak 800 nits, SDR 300 nits. The little square moves only with the images the TV shows, but your screen shows at most as many as it can (usually 60 per second). Limits of 8 px and 20 ms chosen for the comparison.
