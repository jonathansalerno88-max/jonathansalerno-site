Two recipes side by side for removing motion blur on a gaming TV. For each one choose the game's frames per second, the TV's refresh rate, interpolation, black frame insertion and HDR; five checks say whether it passes an online shooter or an HDR adventure.

Starting recipes

Hz = 1/s · fps = images/s · nit = cd/m²

Simplified model, orders of magnitude for a mid-range OLED TV. Persistence P: how long each image stays lit; with no tricks it is 1/fps, with interpolation 1/Hz, with BFI half the refresh. Blur = speed × P, with an object crossing 1,920 px in 2 s (960 px/s). Game-mode lag about 10 ms with a 60 Hz signal and 5 ms at 120 Hz, as in typical RTINGS measurements; low-latency interpolation (only some TVs, and without VRR) must wait for the next real frame, so it adds 1/fps; standard interpolation, outside game mode, brings lag to tens or hundreds of ms (100 here). BFI: half the refresh is black, so half the light and about half a refresh of extra lag; at 60 Hz many people see it flicker, and many TVs switch it off with HDR. If the game is slower than the TV, BFI flashes the same image twice and you see it double. HDR peak 800 nits, SDR 300 nits; convincing HDR from 600 nits up (like the VESA DisplayHDR 600 tier). Blur and lag limits chosen for the comparison: shooter 8 px and 20 ms, adventure 12 px and 50 ms.
