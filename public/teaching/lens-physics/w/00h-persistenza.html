Six screens compared over 50 milliseconds: CRT at 60 Hz, plasma at 60 Hz, OLED at 60 Hz, OLED at 120 Hz, OLED at 120 Hz with black frame insertion and an OLED monitor at 720 Hz. For each, on the left when the image is lit, in the middle the streak it leaves on the retina while the eye follows an object at 960 pixels per second, on the right how the moving object looks.

Goal · see why what matters is how long each image stays lit, not how many hertz the screen has. The eye follows an object at 960 px/s; each lit image slides across the retina and leaves a streak.

Persistence P = how long each image stays lit. Streak on the retina = speed × P (pixels/s × s = pixels). Slow motion shows 50 ms in 6 s, that is 120 times slower. The streaks in the middle are magnified 8 times; in the last column the object is blurred by the real streak, in pixels.
