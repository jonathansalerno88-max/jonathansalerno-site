For each, on the left when the image is lit, in the middle the streak it leaves on the retina while the eye follows an object at 960 pixels per second, then how the moving object looks and finally how black the screen gets in a dark room. Goal · see why what matters is how long each image stays lit, not how many hertz the screen has. The eye follows an object at 960 px/s; each lit image slides across the retina and leaves a streak.

Try it yourself

Persistence P = how long each image stays lit. Streak on the retina = speed × P (pixels/s × s = pixels). In LCDs (QLED and Mini-LED) the pixel transition, here ≈ 5 ms as an indicative value for a VA panel, adds about half its duration. Slow motion shows 50 ms in 6 s, that is 120 times slower. The streaks in the middle are magnified 8 times; in the last column the object is blurred by the real streak, in pixels. Black: in a dark room, with white at about 150 nit (nit = cd/m²); indicative values, and the greys in the boxes are exaggerated to make them visible. The CRT glow is light bouncing inside the thick glass of the screen; the Mini-LED halo is the zone of LEDs that stays lit around the bright object (blooming).
