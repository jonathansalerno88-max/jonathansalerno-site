Comparison seen from above. On the left, Einthoven's string galvanometer: a silver-coated quartz string between the poles of a magnet; the heart's current runs through it and the Lorentz force bends it; its shadow, projected on moving photographic paper, traces the electrocardiogram. On the right, the SEM scan coils: the current in the coils makes the field, which bends the electron beam and moves the spot on the sample. Same law, F = q · v × B; electrons are negative and deflect the other way.

Coil current

The field is fixed: the magnet provides it. What changes is the heart's current, which runs through the string and bends it. From the deflection you read the current: it measures.

You choose the current: it changes the field, and the field bends the beam. With the current you decide where the spot goes: it steers. Electrons are negative, so with the same current and the same field they deflect the other way.
