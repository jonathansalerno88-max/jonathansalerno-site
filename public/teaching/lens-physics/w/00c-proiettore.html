Simplified model, indicative numbers. Matte surface: L = ρ · E / π, with E in lux (lx = lm/m²). Brightness needed: at least 48 nits (the DCI digital-cinema standard) and, with the lights on, at least 3 times the luminance of the wall, because the eye adapts to the room. Contrast needed: 10:1, a threshold chosen here for a watchable picture. Screen: white ρ 0.9, light grey 0.5, ALR 0.8 for projector light and 0.15 for room light. Displays (full-screen nits, reflectance, native contrast): CRT 120 nits, 6%, 10,000:1; plasma 150 nits, 4%, 5,000:1; LED 400 nits, 1.5%, 3,000:1; mini-LED 900 nits, 1.5%, 20,000:1; OLED 250 nits, 1%, near-perfect black. Projector: native contrast 1,000:1 (high-end projectors go much higher). Black: green below 0.05 nits. Burn-in: high risk for plasma, medium for OLED and CRT, none for LED, mini-LED and projector. The ‘How you see black’ panel shows black on a log scale relative to white, with a halo round the star for mini-LED (blooming). Prices are made up and only for comparison.
