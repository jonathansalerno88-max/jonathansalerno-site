Parallel rays enter the eye through the pupil. You choose the defect (blur of a presbyope reading, spherical aberration, chromatic aberration) and the pupil diameter: the peripheral rays stopped by the iris are the ones that err the most, so the spot on the retina shrinks, while the light entering falls with the area.

Take-home points
  • Presbyope: constricting the pupil shrinks the spot but does not move the focus. The real correction is reading glasses.
  • Constrict too much (below about 2 mm): diffraction takes over, the spot grows again and the light drops.
  • Spherical aberration: constricting removes almost all of it (it falls as D³). In lenses it is corrected with aspheric surfaces.
  • Chromatic aberration: constricting reduces it (it falls as D), and the central fovea barely sees blue. In optics it is corrected by pairing two glasses (an achromatic doublet).
  • Healthy eye: the best pupil is about 2.5–3 mm.

Panels at true scale: 8 arcminutes of fovea, one cone every 2.5 µm. Diffraction is calculated; the defect spots are indicative. Colour = how much light, from dark red to white; each panel has its own exposure.
