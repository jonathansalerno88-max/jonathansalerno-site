1 · What a transporter is

The membrane is a lipid bilayer: on their own, only small uncharged molecules get through. Everything else, ions, glucose, amino acids, even water in useful amounts, needs a protein. There are two questions to ask: which protein (channel, carrier or pump) and who pays, that is, whether the substance goes down or up its electrochemical gradient.

2 · The transporters of the kidney

Every transporter of the nephron is classified with one question: does the substance go down or up its electrochemical gradient? If it goes down, transport is passive, across the membrane (simple diffusion) or through a channel or a carrier (facilitated diffusion). If it goes up, energy is needed: the pump spends ATP directly (primary active), or the same carrier uses another ion that goes down, usually Na⁺ (secondary active: symport if they go the same way, antiport if opposite). Channels such as ENaC, ROMK and aquaporins are always passive.

The whole kidney, with the arterioles, the RAAS and the sympathetic system, is on the page about portal circulations.
