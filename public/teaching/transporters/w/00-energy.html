A substance runs downhill in free energy by itself; to make it go up something else must go down further: passive, primary active, secondary active.

  1. Every cell exchanges matter with what surrounds it. In doing so it cannot break three balances: of mass, of charge and of energy. Nothing is created and nothing is destroyed: what comes in equals what goes out, plus what builds up.

The three fundamental balances: what comes in equals what goes out

1ṁin = ṁoutmass: this is Lavoisier's principle (1789)

1aṅi,in + Ri = ṅi,outmass, looked at one substance at a time. Ri = the moles that have not entered, but are produced inside that compartment; if they are used up instead, it is negative

2Σi zi ṅi,in = Σi zi ṅi,outcharge: as many charges come in as go out

3Ėin = Ėoutenergy

3aUA , UBstart here: each compartment has its own internal energy U, that of molecular agitation and of chemical bonds [M L² T⁻²], J (J = kg·m²/s²)

3bH = U + P·Vbut it is more correct to use enthalpy H: to internal energy it adds P·V, the energy it took to make room for the matter at that pressure. This way volume work (PV) is accounted for once and for all: it is negligible in a cell, which does not change volume; fundamental in a piston and in the heart, whose work is exactly the area of the pressure-volume loop

3cHbefore = q + wnon PV + Hafterthen we transfer matter from A to B. The enthalpy missing at the end has been exchanged as heat (q) or as non-PV work (electrical, transport, mechanical): −ΔH = q + wnon PV. For the whole human this is the balance Atwater measured in 1899: The greats of medicine →

Of this ΔH, how much can go into non-PV work and how much must go into heat? The balance does not say: any split suits it, even all of it as heat.

Every transporter always obeys these three balances. But the balances do not yet say which way the substance goes, nor how ΔH is split between heat and work: glucose coming in and glucose going out both obey them. To know it, one more concept is needed: entropy.

  1. How much of ΔH must go into heat: entropy

    1qmin = − T·ΔSthe compulsory share: at least this much heat must be exchanged with the surroundings, and cannot become work. If the entropy of the system goes down (ΔS < 0) it is heat to give away: the price of order. If it goes up (ΔS > 0) the sign flips, and the system can take heat from the surroundings

    2wnon PV, max = − ΔH − qmin = − (ΔH − T·ΔS)what is left of ΔH, once the compulsory share is taken out, is the most that can come out as non-PV work

    3ΔG = ΔH − T·ΔS  →  wnon PV, max = − ΔGthis remainder has a name: Gibbs free energy. It is, by definition, the maximum non-PV work a process can deliver at constant temperature and pressure

    And it also gives the direction: a process happens by itself if it can deliver work, that is, if ΔG < 0.

    • S = entropy [M L² T⁻² Θ⁻¹], J/K (J = kg·m²/s²) · T = absolute temperature [Θ], K · T·ΔS = an energy [M L² T⁻²], J
    • q, wnon PV, ΔH, ΔG = energies [M L² T⁻²], J
    Provisional notes

    To pick up again: the chemical potential μ, that is, G for a single substance.

    4μ~ = μ° + R T ln c + z F φthe electrochemical potential of a substance: a chemical part (concentration) and an electrical part (charge × voltage)

    5ΔG = μ~B − μ~A = R T ln (cB / cA) + z F (φB − φA)going from A to B: if ΔG < 0 the substance goes by itself (passive); if ΔG > 0 someone must pay (active)

    6G = Σi ni μiG of a mixture: each substance contributes its moles times its μ

    • ΔG, μ~, μ° = free energy, electrochemical potential, standard chemical potential, all per mole [M L² T⁻² N⁻¹], J/mol (J = kg·m²/s²)
    • R = gas constant, 8.314 J/(mol·K) [M L² T⁻² Θ⁻¹ N⁻¹] · T = absolute temperature [Θ], K
    • c = concentration, relative to 1 mol/L: a pure number · z = charge of the ion, a pure number
    • F = Faraday constant, 96 485 C/mol [T I N⁻¹] (C = A·s) · φ = electric potential [M L² T⁻³ I⁻¹], V (V = J/C)
    Supplementary note: quantifying entropy starting from U

    If you open U you find the energy levels of the molecules: each one, at every instant, occupies one level (electronic, vibrational, rotational, translational). U is the total: for each level, how many molecules sit on it times the energy of that level, plus the energy of the interactions between molecules.

    aU = Σi Ni Ei + Uinteractions

    The same U can be obtained by distributing the molecules over the levels in a great many different ways. The number of these ways is W, and entropy measures it:

    bS = k ln W

    • U = internal energy [M L² T⁻²], J · Ni = number of molecules on level i, a pure number · Ei = energy of level i [M L² T⁻²], J
    • W = number of ways of distributing the molecules over the levels, a pure number · k = Boltzmann constant, 1.381 × 10⁻²³ J/K [M L² T⁻² Θ⁻¹]

    U says how much energy there is; S says in how many ways it can be distributed. More ways, more entropy.
