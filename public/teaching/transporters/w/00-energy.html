A substance runs downhill in free energy by itself; to make it go up something else must go down further: passive, primary active, secondary active.

  1. Every cell exchanges matter with what surrounds it. In doing so it cannot break three balances: of mass, of charge and of energy. Nothing is created and nothing is destroyed: what comes in equals what goes out, plus what builds up.

The three fundamental balances: what comes in equals what goes out

1ṁin = ṁoutmass: this is Lavoisier's principle (1789)

1aṅi,in + Ri = ṅi,outmass, looked at one substance at a time. Ri = the moles that have not entered, but are produced inside that compartment; if they are used up instead, it is negative

2Σi zi ṅi,in = Σi zi ṅi,outcharge: as many charges come in as go out

This is the balance Atwater measured in humans in 1899: The greats of medicine →

3bẇ = ẇPV + ẇnon PVwork, split in two. ẇPV = volume work, the boundary expanding against a pressure: fundamental in a piston, negligible in a cell, which does not change volume. ẇnon PV = everything else, that is electrical, transport and mechanical work: in the cell this is the one that counts

Every transporter always obeys these three balances. But the balances do not yet say which way the substance goes: glucose coming in and glucose going out both obey them. To know the direction one more concept is needed: entropy.
