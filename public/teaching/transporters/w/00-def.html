Two environments A and B separated by a barrier: the transporter lets one substance through, the receptor lets only a signal through.

  1. There is no agreed definition of what life is.9,10
  2. But all the life we know has one thing in common: it is made of cells, that is, of environments kept apart from the outside by a boundary.11,12
  3. A completely sealed-off cell, however, has never been observed: all the ones we know exchange matter with what surrounds them, at least at some stage of their life.
  4. A transporter is a biological structure that lets one specific substance pass from one environment to the other, without undoing the separation.
  5. A receptor, instead, lets only information through: the molecule stays where it is, and a signal arrives on the other side.

A and B are any two places: outside and inside the cell, the blood and a tissue, the cytosol and the mitochondrion.
