A tank split by a membrane with a K⁺ channel and a Cl⁻ channel, with a voltmeter; adding negatively charged proteins that cannot cross, K⁺ and Cl⁻ redistribute (Donnan equilibrium) and the inside keeps more particles; if water can cross, the membrane bulges until it tears, unless a wall pushes back or there is a non-diffusible ion outside too (double Donnan). And what if we put a diffusible ion and a non-diffusible ion together?

The Donnan effect in the tank, with a Gamblegram that follows it

Electroneutrality must always be respected: on each side cations = anions, column by column.

[ ]in = concentrations inside, [ ]out = outside [N L⁻³], mM = mmol/L = mol/m³ · [A⁻] = negative charges of the proteins, which do not cross [N L⁻³], mM · [Na⁺]out = the non-diffusible ion put outside [N L⁻³], mM · x = KCl that leaves [N L⁻³], mM · KCl added outside: doses of 50 mM, up to 150 mM · osm = osmolality, dissolved particles per kg of water [N M⁻¹], mOsm/kg (≈ mOsm/L; 1 mOsm/L = 1 mol/m³) · r = Donnan ratio [pure number] · Vm = membrane potential, inside − outside [M L² T⁻³ I⁻¹], mV (V = J/C) · ΔP = turgor pressure [M L⁻¹ T⁻²], Pa (Pa = N/m² = kg/(m·s²)); 1 atm = 101,325 Pa, 1 mmHg = 133.3 Pa · R = gas constant, 8.314 J/(mol·K) (J = kg·m²/s²) · T = absolute temperature [Θ], 310 K · EK, ECl = Nernst potentials [M L² T⁻³ I⁻¹], mV · n = particles of one side, osm · V [N], mOsm (per kg of water at the start) · 61.5 mV = 2.303 · RT/F at 37 °C · volume inside = relative to the start [pure number], %. Gamblegram: for each side, cations on the left and anions on the right; the two columns are equally tall (electroneutrality) and their sum is the osmolality. Simplifications: ideal solutions; each protein charge counts as one particle; the tank is closed, so water that enters one side leaves the other; ions settle faster than water; while ions move the voltmeter uses the GHK equation with PK = PCl (at equilibrium the value does not depend on the permeabilities); each symbol is 10 mM.
